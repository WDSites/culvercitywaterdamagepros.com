Storms, Ballona Creek and indoor water

Ballona Creek runs through Culver City and has been a concrete flood control channel since 1935, according to the city. In February 2024 the city issued a storm notice that warned of peak rain rates of half an inch to an inch an hour and listed sandbag locations at Fire Station One, the headquarters station at 9600 Culver Blvd and the City Yard.

Rain is only one cause of indoor water. Supply lines, water heaters, washing machines, air conditioner drains and roofs all fail on dry days too. The Culver City Fire Department advises residents not to enter flooded below-grade garages, so call 911 for any life safety risk first and then call our line.

Know Before You Call

What Sets the Price of a Restoration Job

Every home is different, so the price depends on what the crew finds. These four factors move it the most, and your crew explains them before work starts.

Water category

Clean water from a supply line costs less to handle than sewage or floodwater.

Affected area

More rooms, more wet wall height and more flooring mean more equipment and labor.

Materials involved

Hardwood, plaster and insulation dry differently from carpet and plain drywall.

Drying and rebuild scope

Drying days, demolition and the repair after drying set the total.

No price is quoted from a web page. Call (424) 550-5662 and describe what you see for your real number.

Insurance and Your Records

We document the loss in writing, with photos, moisture readings and a scope of work you can share with your insurer. Coverage questions are answered by your insurer and your policy. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage.

  • Photos of every wet surface before anything is moved.
  • Moisture readings and a drying log from the crew.
  • A written scope and price before work begins.
  • A list of materials removed and replaced.
