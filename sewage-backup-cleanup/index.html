Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Culver City, CA

Sewage water carries bacteria and viruses, so a backup is a health problem as well as a flooding one. The safe first move is to keep people and pets out of the area and stop using drains. This page explains what a sewage cleanup involves and how it differs from clean water work.

What a sewage backup is and where it comes from

A sewage backup happens when wastewater cannot leave the building and returns through the lowest fixture, usually a floor drain, a shower, a tub or a toilet. A blockage in the building's own drain, a root intrusion or a problem further along the line can cause it.

Who is responsible for the blockage depends on where it sits. If you are unsure, ask a plumber to scope the line and ask your property manager or the city's public works contact before assuming. We do not diagnose the line. We clean what came out of it.

First hour safety steps for a sewage backup

Sewage is not a do-it-yourself cleanup.

  • Keep children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system out of the area.
  • Stop running water, flushing toilets and using washers until the line is cleared.
  • Do not walk through the water barefoot or touch it without waterproof gloves and boots.
  • Turn off power to the area if water is near outlets.
  • Call a plumber for the blockage and call dispatch for the cleanup.

Health guidance and why porous materials go

The EPA's guidance for flooded homes treats sewage and floodwater as hazardous to health and says porous materials soaked by contaminated water are usually discarded rather than cleaned. That includes carpet, pad, insulation and wet drywall.

Hard surfaces can be cleaned and disinfected, and the crew separates what can be saved from what cannot. The Building Safety Division's permit list says interior clean up needs no permit, but the plumbing repair might, so ask your plumber.

How the cleanup runs after a sewage backup

The crew works with protective gear and containment.

  1. Contain and protect. The crew wears protective gear and seals the area so contamination does not spread to clean rooms.
  2. Extract contaminated water. Pumps and wet vacuums remove the water, and the waste is bagged for disposal.
  3. Remove porous materials. Carpet, pad, insulation and wet board are cut out and removed.
  4. Clean and disinfect. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and treated with disinfectant.
  5. Dry and verify. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings are normal.

What changes the cost of a sewage cleanup

Black water jobs cost more than clean water jobs because of protective gear, disposal and the amount of material removed. The depth of the water, the floor type and how long it sat all change the scope.

The crew explains the scope and price on site before work begins.

Insurance and sewage backup

Many homeowner policies exclude sewer or drain backup unless an endorsement was added. Ask your insurer whether you have one before cleanup, and take photos of everything before removal.

Renters and condo owners should also tell the landlord or association right away, since the cause may sit in a shared line.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

These habits make backups worse or more dangerous.

  • Using a household wet vacuum on sewage.
  • Keeping wet carpet because it looks clean once dry.
  • Pouring grease down drains.
  • Flushing wipes labeled as flushable.
  • Ignoring a slow drain that returns after clearing.
