Water Damage Cleanup in Sunkist Park, Culver City, CA

Sunkist Park is the southwest Culver City neighborhood that sits beside Ballona Creek, and in a heavy storm its streets have had to carry a lot of water. This page explains what that can mean for a home, what to do in the first hour and how cleanup and drying work. It also covers the ordinary causes, such as a failed supply line or a water heater.

Where Sunkist Park sits

Sunkist Park, also known as El Marino, is one of the 15 neighborhoods on the City of Culver City's official neighborhoods map. It lies in the southwest part of the city, and it is commonly described as bounded by Ballona Creek, Sepulveda Boulevard, Jefferson Boulevard and Playa Street. Those edges are approximate, and the city's map says it is for representational purposes only.

The neighborhood is residential, with streets of single-unit homes around El Marino School. Its position beside the creek channel is the main geographic fact that shapes how storm water behaves here.

What happened on McDonald Street in February 2024

Culver City Crossroads, a local news outlet, reported on February 5, 2024 that after almost six inches of rain, water backed up from Ballona Creek into a storm drain at the McDonald Street cul-de-sac. By about 6 pm it had risen to the top of the curb and was reaching driveways and lawns.

Public Works then set up a diesel pump to move the overflow, and a city crew manager described the inflow as almost more than the system could handle.

The report describes water in the street, not water inside houses, and we have not found a record of indoor damage to individual homes. It still shows how storm water and the sewer system can be stressed at the same time in this neighborhood.

First hour safety steps

Storm water and drain water are both treated as contaminated until proven otherwise.

  • Stay out of standing water if outlets, cords or appliances may be touching it.
  • Do not wade through street water, because it can hide holes and carry debris.
  • Keep children and pets away from any water that came up through a drain.
  • Call 911 for downed power lines, a gas smell or any immediate danger.
  • Take photographs from a dry spot, then call dispatch and your plumber.

How cleanup and drying work in a Sunkist Park home

The steps are the same for a garage entry or a plumbing leak, with extra care when the water is contaminated.

  1. Make it safe. The crew checks for electrical hazards and decides how to protect the rest of the house.
  2. Extract water. Pumps and wet vacuums remove standing water, and contaminated water is handled with protective gear.
  3. Remove what cannot dry. Soaked carpet pad, insulation and wet drywall that contacted contaminated water are cut out.
  4. Dry and measure. Air movers and dehumidifiers run while moisture readings are logged until the structure is dry.
  5. Document the loss. Photographs and readings are kept so you can share them with your insurer.

What changes the cost

The main factors are the water category, the area affected and the materials involved. Clean supply water is simpler than water that came up from a drain. A garage slab dries differently from carpet over a wood subfloor.

No price is quoted from a web page. A crew explains scope and price on site after seeing the damage.

Insurance and flood status

Insurers separate sudden plumbing failure, sewer backup and surface flooding. Standard homeowner policies often treat surface flooding as excluded and may exclude sewer backup unless an endorsement was added. Read your policy and ask your insurer how the cause is classified.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status has not been verified for Sunkist Park. You can check your own address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center or ask your insurance agent. A mapped zone, or its absence, is not a prediction of what water will do in a storm.
