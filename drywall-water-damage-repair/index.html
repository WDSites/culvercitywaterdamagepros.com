Wall and Drywall Water Damage Repair in Culver City, CA

Drywall soaks up water like a sponge, and once it is soft it cannot be saved. The question is how much to cut out and when the cavity behind it is dry enough to close. This page explains how wet walls are judged, opened, dried and repaired.

How walls get wet

This page is about the wall itself. Water gets into walls from pipes inside the cavity, from leaks above that run down, from floors that wick water up and from storms driving rain through exterior wall seams. The drywall paper takes it in, the gypsum softens and the stud cavity stays wet long after the surface looks fine.

A wall can look intact and still hold water. Insulation inside the cavity acts like a sponge, which is why moisture readings come before any cutting.

First hour safety steps with a wet wall

Wall cavities carry wiring and pipes.

  • Turn off power to circuits that run through the wet wall.
  • Close the water main if a pipe may be leaking.
  • Do not cut into a wall without knowing what runs behind it.
  • Stay out from under a ceiling or wall that is sagging.
  • Photograph the wall and call dispatch.

How far to cut

Crews commonly cut drywall higher than the visible stain, because wicking carries water above the wet line, and use moisture readings to decide the height. That is a field judgment, not a fixed rule, and the readings decide.

Opening a wall also lets air reach the cavity. For the drying method, see structural drying. For wall repair in general, the Building Safety Division's published permit list says interior clean up needs no permit, but you should confirm your repair at (310) 253-5800.

How wall repair runs

Repair begins only after the wall reads dry.

  1. Find the source. The crew confirms the leak is fixed and photographs the wall.
  2. Map moisture. Meters and a thermal camera show the wet height and any wet insulation.
  3. Cut and remove. Wet board and insulation are removed, and the cavity is opened.
  4. Dry the cavity. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until the studs and plate read dry.
  5. Patch and finish. New board, tape, texture and paint match the room once readings are done.

What changes the cost of wall repair

Cost follows the area of wet drywall, the height of the cut, whether insulation is wet and the finish. Texture matching and paint across a whole wall add work.

Walls that share a cavity with plumbing or electrical lines may need other trades first. Scope and price are explained on site once the wet height is read.

Insurance and wall damage

Sudden leaks are often covered and slow leaks often are not. Keep photographs of the stain before cutting and ask for the moisture readings, because they show the extent.

If a plumber found the cause, keep the invoice with the claim records.

Mistakes to avoid

Wall repairs fail when the cavity was not dry.

  • Painting over a stain with stain-blocking primer only.
  • Closing a wall the same day water was found.
  • Leaving wet insulation inside the cavity.
  • Cutting before checking for pipes and wires.
  • Matching paint before the wall is dry.
