AC Leak Water Damage Repair in Culver City, CA

An air conditioner leak is usually a clogged condensate drain line, a cracked drain pan or a frozen coil that melts and overflows. The water often shows up as a ceiling stain, a wet closet floor or a damp wall near the air handler. Stop the system, find the source, dry the materials and then repair the ceiling, wall or floor. This guide walks through each step.

By the Culver City Water Damage Pros team · October 27, 2026 · Leaks

Why air conditioners leak

An air conditioner pulls moisture from the air as it cools. That water, called condensate, collects in a pan and drains away through a line. If the line clogs with algae or debris, the pan overflows and the water goes where gravity sends it.

A frozen evaporator coil causes a similar problem. When it thaws, it can melt more water than the pan can handle. Other causes include a cracked pan, a loose connection or a failed float switch that is supposed to shut the system down.

What to do when you find an AC leak

Move fast and keep it safe.

  1. Turn off the system. Switch off the air conditioner at the thermostat and, if you can safely reach it, at the breaker.
  2. Stop the water. Place a bucket or towels under drips and remove standing water from the floor.
  3. Clear the drain line. A technician can flush or vacuum the condensate line, and some homeowners use a wet vacuum on the outside end.
  4. Call for drying. Wet drywall and insulation need drying. Do not close up the area.
  5. Document damage. Photograph the stain, the unit and the pan for your records.

Drying and repair after an AC leak

Condensate water is usually clean at the source, but it travels through insulation and dust and can leave stains and smells. Wet drywall and insulation are removed if they are soft, while framing is dried in place.

The repair that follows is the same rebuild used for any ceiling or wall: new drywall, texture and paint. The water damage repair page explains how scope and price are set. If water also reached carpet or flooring, water damage cleanup covers removal and sanitizing.

Preventing repeat AC leaks

Most AC leaks are preventable with simple upkeep.

  • Have the condensate line flushed during regular HVAC service.
  • Check that the drain pan is clean, level and not cracked.
  • Replace air filters on schedule to prevent coil freezing.
  • Ask your technician to install a float switch or a secondary pan if none exists.
  • Watch for ceiling stains and water at the air handler after heavy use.

Insurance and AC leaks

Coverage for an AC leak depends on your policy and the cause. A sudden failure is often treated as accidental damage, while slow leaks from neglected upkeep often are not. The California Department of Insurance lists wear and tear among the perils that are usually excluded and advises reading your policy.

Take photos, keep the HVAC technician's invoice and share it with your insurer. If you are unsure, ask before repairs begin.

What a technician checks during an AC service

A service visit usually includes clearing the condensate line, checking the drain pan, inspecting the coil and verifying the float switch. Technicians also check airflow and filters, since poor airflow is a common cause of frozen coils.

Ask the technician to show you where the drain line ends and to confirm that water flows freely. Keep the service report with your records. It shows you maintained the system, which can matter if you later file an insurance claim.

Culver City permits for mechanical and water heater work

The Building Safety Division's permit page says permits apply to repair of plumbing, electrical, gas and mechanical systems, and its plumbing list names water heater replacement as needing one. It lists stopping leaks and clearing clogs without replacing pipe as not needing one.

Ask your HVAC contractor whether a permit applies to your repair, and confirm with the division at (310) 253-5800 if needed. Properties in Marina del Rey follow county permit rules, so ask the county.
