Where Fox Hills sits and what it is

Fox Hills is one of the 15 neighborhoods on the City of Culver City's official neighborhoods map. It is commonly described as a roughly triangular area bounded by Slauson Avenue, Centinela Avenue, the San Diego Freeway and Canterbury Drive. Those edges are approximate, since the city's map is for representational purposes only.

Reference sources describe the area as annexed to Culver City in 1964 and built out with apartments and condominiums in the 1970s. Fox Hills Mall opened in 1975 on the site of a golf course, and Culver City Crossroads reports that the Fox Hills Neighborhood Association was formed in 1972. The housing here is mostly multi-unit.

First hour safety steps

In a condo or apartment, the shutoff and the breaker may be shared.

  • Find your unit's water shutoff, which is often under a sink or at the water heater, and close it.
  • Turn off power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and easy to reach.
  • Stay out from under ceilings that sag or drip near light fixtures.
  • Tell the property manager or association right away, because the source may be in another unit.
  • Take photographs of every wet surface before anything is moved.

The stormwater project at Fox Hills Park

A Safe Clean Water Program planning document describes a proposed stormwater capture project at Fox Hills Park, where runoff would be diverted from a city storm drain on Buckingham Parkway to an underground infiltration structure. Its stated goals are water quality under Ballona Creek requirements and reducing flood risk, and the document lists the project as being in the planning phase.

The document does not describe flooding in Fox Hills homes, and a proposed project is not a record of damage. We mention it only as local context, and we do not claim Fox Hills floods.

How cleanup works in a multi-unit building

The extra step in a shared building is records that other people can read.

  1. Confirm the source is off. The crew checks that the water supply for the leak is closed.
  2. Extract and map. Pumps remove standing water, and meters and a thermal camera map wet walls and ceilings in each unit.
  3. Open and dry. Baseboards and wall sections are opened where readings call for it, and equipment runs with daily readings.
  4. Document each space. Photographs and readings are recorded per room so a landlord, association and insurers can each see their part.
  5. Hand off the repair. Once dry, the rebuild is scoped. See water damage repair.

What changes the cost

The number of affected units, the wet area and the number of drying days set most of the scope. Water that crossed a floor into a second unit means more rooms to dry and more records to keep.

A crew explains scope and price on site once the damage is read.

Insurance, associations and renters

A condo owner may have both a personal policy and an association policy, and which one responds depends on where the damage is and what the governing documents say. Renters and owners should read their own policy and ask their insurer or agent. We do not decide coverage.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status has not been verified for Fox Hills. Most Fox Hills water losses start inside a building, so plumbing and appliance coverage questions come up more often than flood questions.
