How to Repair Water Damaged Wood in Culver City, CA

To repair water damaged wood, dry it first, test it for soundness and then decide between repair and replacement. Wood that is firm after drying can often be sanded, filled and refinished. Wood that is soft, crumbling or stained black is usually rotted and needs to be cut out. This guide covers structural framing, trim, cabinets and furniture, and the point where a homeowner should call a pro.

By the Culver City Water Damage Pros team · October 27, 2026 · Repair

Start by drying, not by sanding

Wood holds water longer than drywall and releases it slowly. Sanding, filling or painting wet wood traps moisture and invites rot or mold. The first step is always to stop the water and dry the wood to a stable moisture level.

A moisture meter is the right tool. Wood framing in a normal home sits at a moderate moisture content, and a crew compares readings in the damaged area with readings in dry areas of the same wood. Ask the crew to share the numbers.

Wood parts and what happens to them

Each part of a home reacts differently to water.

Structural framing

Studs, joists and plates are dried in place when sound. Rotted sections are repaired by sistering or replacing.

Baseboards and trim

These often swell and are replaced, because matching profile and paint is simple and cheap compared with refinishing.

Cabinets

Solid wood bases can dry flat, while particle board swells and is replaced.

Furniture

Solid pieces can be dried slowly and refinished. Veneer lifts and is harder to save.

Drying wood without damaging it

Wood dries best when the air around it is dry and moving, not hot. Dehumidifiers and air movers are used, and heat is avoided because fast drying splits or warps boards. For framing, small openings in drywall or baseboard let air reach the wood.

Dry slowly and recheck. If wood stays wet because of a leak you have not found, repairs will fail. The water mitigation page explains how drying is planned and measured, and why the crew returns to take readings.

Dry rot and why it needs fast attention

Dry rot is a decay fungus that weakens wood that has stayed damp over time. It leaves wood brittle, cracked and cubed, and it can spread through moist wood. Fixing the moisture source and replacing decayed sections are the only long-term fixes.

If you find rot in framing near a shower, a window or a roof edge, bring in a repair crew. A rebuild after rot is not a cosmetic patch, and permit questions may apply. Water damage repair follows drying and source repair, as the repair page explains.

Before you pick up tools

Safety and sequence matter more than speed.

  • Confirm the water source is stopped and the wood is dry.
  • Wear gloves, a mask and eye protection when cutting or sanding old, damp wood.
  • Check for electrical wiring and plumbing inside the wall before cutting.
  • Do not paint over wood until it has been dry for the time a crew recommends.
  • Photograph the damage before repair for your insurer.

Choosing a repair method

Small dents and surface stains can often be sanded and refinished once the wood is dry. Splits and soft spots may need epoxy consolidators or a patch piece, which a carpenter can cut to match the grain. Heavily rotted members need replacement, sometimes with a sistered new board alongside the old one.

The right method depends on the load the wood carries. Trim and cabinets forgive cosmetic repairs. Framing and subfloors do not, and a structural question belongs to a licensed professional who can look at it in person.

Culver City permits for rot and wood repair

The Building Safety Division lists termite or dry rot repair, shower wall repair or enclosures and wall replacement among repairs that need a permit. It lists painting and minor sheetrock repair as work that does not. Those lines matter when decayed framing is found behind a shower or a wall.

Call (310) 253-5800 before work begins. Permit rules are set by the city, so we point you to them instead of guessing. Coastal addresses in Marina del Rey follow county rules.
