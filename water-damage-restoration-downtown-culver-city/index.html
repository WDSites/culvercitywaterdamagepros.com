What Downtown Culver City is

Downtown Culver City is one of the 15 neighborhoods on the City of Culver City's official neighborhoods map. It is the city's commercial and civic center, and it mixes shops, restaurants, offices, studios and housing. The city's map is for representational purposes only, so the edges of the district are approximate.

Reference sources describe a downtown revitalization program in the early 1990s and newer mixed-use development such as The Culver Steps. Older landmarks stand beside that newer construction, and the contrast matters for water damage.

Landmarks and what they tell us about age

The Los Angeles Conservancy lists the Culver Hotel at 9400 Culver Boulevard as built in 1924, designed by Curlett and Beelman and operating today as a 46-room hotel. Buildings of that age were built with different materials and layouts than recent construction, and that affects how water travels and how long materials take to dry.

We do not claim any specific plumbing material or incident for any building. We describe the pattern only, and a crew reads each building on site.

First hour safety steps

Protect people first, then property, and let the building manager know.

  • Clear the area of staff, customers and residents before anything else.
  • Close the nearest water shutoff, and ask the building manager about the main.
  • Turn off power to wet circuits, and call the building engineer if there is one.
  • Move records, electronics and stock away from the water if it is safe to do so.
  • Photograph each affected space, then call dispatch.

How cleanup works in a downtown building

Coordination is half of the job, because several people share one structure.

  1. Assess and secure. The crew finds the source, checks hazards and notes which spaces share walls or floors.
  2. Extract water. Pumps and wet vacuums clear floors, and wet carpet or tile is lifted where water collected below.
  3. Map moisture. Moisture meters and a thermal camera show which walls, subfloors and slab areas are holding water.
  4. Dry the space. Air movers and dehumidifiers are sized to the area, and readings are logged.
  5. Share records. Readings and photographs go to you for your insurer, landlord or tenant.

What changes the cost

The floor area, the number of affected spaces, the floor type and the drying days set most of the scope. A leak that crosses from a shop into a unit above or below means more records and more equipment.

Insurance and who is involved

A tenant, a landlord and an association can each carry a different policy, and each responds to different parts of a loss. Read your policy and lease and ask your insurer or agent which applies. We do not decide coverage.
