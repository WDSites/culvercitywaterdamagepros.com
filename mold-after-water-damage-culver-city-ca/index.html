Mold After Water Damage in Culver City, CA: Timeline and Next Steps

Mold can follow water damage when materials stay wet. The EPA says water-damaged areas and items should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and that the key to mold control is moisture control. That is why the first two days after a leak or flood decide so much. This guide explains how mold develops, what to watch for and how to decide when to call a crew.

By the Culver City Water Damage Pros team · October 27, 2026 · Mold

How mold follows water

Mold spores are everywhere. They need moisture, a food source such as drywall paper or wood and time to grow. Water damage supplies the moisture, and building materials supply the food.

That is why a flood or a long-running leak raises the risk, and why a one-time spill that dries quickly usually does not cause trouble. The problem is not the water. It is how long the water stays.

The first days: what to do

This sequence cuts the risk in most cases.

  1. Stop the source. Fix or shut off the leak so no new water arrives.
  2. Remove standing water. Pumps and extraction pull the bulk of it out within hours.
  3. Dry wet materials. Dehumidifiers and air movers dry carpet, drywall and wood. Items that cannot be dried are removed.
  4. Check hidden areas. Moisture meters check wall cavities and subfloors.
  5. Watch for signs. Smell, staining and visible growth can appear days later.

When to call for inspection or remediation

Call if you can see growth, if a smell persists after the area is dry or if materials stayed wet beyond two days. Do not wait for the problem to grow. If water is still present, call the 24/7 line first and ask for water mitigation.

Mold remediation removes affected materials and cleans the area. Mold remediation explains containment, removal, cleaning and drying. Testing is a separate service and can include independent clearance after the work.

What not to do

Do not paint over mold, and do not scrub dry mold, which can spread spores. Do not rely on bleach alone, and do not run a fan across growth. Keep the area closed while you arrange help.

Health questions belong to a physician. A contractor cannot diagnose or promise outcomes, and mold advice online is no substitute for medical advice.

Insurance and mold

Coverage for mold depends on its cause. Many policies cover mold that follows a covered sudden loss, and many exclude mold from long-term moisture. Flood insurance typically does not cover mold or mildew damage that could have been avoided, according to the California Department of Insurance.

Document the water event, the drying steps and the dates. Records show that you acted quickly, and they help your insurer understand the timeline.

A two-week watch plan after a leak

Mold can start in the first days, so keep watching after the room feels dry. In the first week, run a dehumidifier, keep air moving and check the area daily for smells or spots. In the second week, check the corners, the back of baseboards and the underside of furniture.

If you see growth, take photos, avoid disturbing it and call for inspection. If everything stays clean and dry, you have likely caught it in time, but a moisture reading is the only way to confirm that hidden materials are dry.

Culver City: where hidden moisture shows up

The city's Building Safety Division lists shower wall repair or enclosures among work that needs a permit, which is a reminder that shower surrounds are a common place for water to hide. If you find mold behind a shower wall, ask the division at (310) 253-5800 about permits before the wall is opened.
