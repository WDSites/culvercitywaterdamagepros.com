Water Damage Ceiling Repair Cost in Culver City, CA: What Sets the Price

The cost of water damage ceiling repair depends on how much drywall is wet, what caused the leak, whether insulation is involved and how hard the texture is to match. No honest price can be given without seeing the ceiling, and this guide does not make one up. It explains the five factors that move the number so you can compare scopes and ask the right questions.

By the Culver City Water Damage Pros team · October 27, 2026 · Cost

Why there is no single ceiling repair price

A small stain from a one-time drip and a collapsed section after a long leak are different jobs. The first may be a patch and a coat of paint. The second involves removing wet drywall and insulation, checking the framing and rebuilding.

Anyone who quotes a flat number without looking at the ceiling is guessing. A written scope after an inspection is the right basis for comparing prices.

Five factors that move the price

These are the things contractors weigh. If a scope leaves one out, ask about it.

Size of the damage

A patch is small. A full room means removing, hanging, taping, texturing and painting a larger surface.

Source of the leak

A roof leak, a plumbing line and an air conditioner drain have different repairs, and the source repair is usually separate.

Insulation and framing

Wet insulation is removed, and framing is checked and dried before new drywall goes up.

Texture and paint matching

Popcorn, knockdown and orange peel textures take different amounts of skill to match.

Access and height

High ceilings, stairs and stored furniture add setup time.

Why the source repair is separate

A ceiling repair does not fix the leak. A plumber repairs a supply line, a roofer repairs a roof and an HVAC technician fixes a condensate drain. Restoration crews dry the structure and rebuild, and those costs usually sit alongside the source repair, not inside it.

Fixing the ceiling before the source is stopped means doing the job twice. If you are not sure where the water came from, a moisture check can trace the path, and water mitigation starts the drying while the source is found.

How to compare ceiling repair scopes

Ask each contractor for the same information. It makes the numbers comparable.

  1. What is removed. Drywall area, insulation and any framing work.
  2. How drying is confirmed. Moisture readings before closing the ceiling.
  3. What is rebuilt. Drywall thickness, taping, texture style and primer.
  4. What is not included. Source repair, paint of adjacent walls and furniture moving.
  5. How the price is set. Fixed price, hourly or by area, and what changes it.

Insurance and ceiling damage

Whether a ceiling repair is covered depends on the cause. A sudden pipe failure is often covered, while a slow leak from poor upkeep often is not. The California Department of Insurance says wear and tear is among the perils that are usually excluded, and it advises reading your policy's exclusions.

If you plan to file a claim, take photos before anything is cut and keep receipts for emergency steps. The water damage repair page covers how the rebuild phase is scoped, and a written scope helps your insurer see what you see.

What to expect on the day of a ceiling repair

A crew starts by protecting floors and furniture, then cuts out the wet or sagging drywall and checks the framing and insulation above. If the area is dry, new board goes in, the seams are taped and the surface is textured to match.

Paint comes last, and it often covers the whole ceiling or at least the whole section to the nearest corner, because a single patch can show. Ask the contractor whether the quote includes full-ceiling paint so there are no surprises.

Questions that keep a quote honest

Ask each contractor the same list.

  • Has the leak source been fixed and by whom?
  • Is the framing and insulation above the ceiling dry?
  • How will the texture be matched?
  • Does the price include paint for the whole ceiling?
  • What happens if the crew finds more damage once the ceiling is open?

Ceiling repair and permits in Culver City

The Building Safety Division's permit list names minor sheetrock repair and painting as work that needs no permit, and it lists replacement of a wall as work that does. A patch is on the exempt side, and a larger rebuild that changes walls may not be. The list is not exhaustive.

Call the division at (310) 253-5800 before you plan a large repair, or when the leak came from pipe replacement. If your ceiling is in Marina del Rey, ask Los Angeles County which office handles your address, because it is unincorporated.
