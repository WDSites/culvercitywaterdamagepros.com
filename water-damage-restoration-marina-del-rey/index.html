Marina del Rey: the community at the mouth of Ballona Creek

Marina del Rey is an unincorporated community of Los Angeles County on the coast, beside a man-made harbor. Ballona Creek, the flood control channel that runs through Culver City, ends here as it reaches Santa Monica Bay, which links the two places in a way that matters during heavy rain.

Our base is in Culver City, so crews drive west for Marina del Rey calls. The area has its own geography, including Mother's Beach, Fisherman's Village and Ballona Lagoon on the Marina Peninsula, and its own water problems. Buildings sit close to sea level, near open water.

When water is spreading in Marina del Rey

Water spreads fast in a multi-unit building. A leak that starts under a sink in one unit can reach the unit below within hours, so tell your property manager as soon as you know. Close the shutoff valve at the fixture, or the main valve for your unit if you can find it.

If the water is gray or brown, or came up through a drain, avoid contact and wait for protective gear. Call 911 for electrical or gas risks before you call us. The Los Angeles County Waterworks Districts, which operate the Marina del Rey water system for the county's Department of Beaches and Harbors, list a water emergency line at 1-800-675-HELP.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading

These steps help whether you live in a single-family home, a condo or an apartment. Skip any that would put you in water near electrical equipment.

Stop the source

Close the supply valve at the fixture, the water heater or the unit's main valve.

Protect power

Do not stand in water near outlets or a panel. Cut power at the panel only if it is dry and reachable.

Lift and move

Raise electronics, rugs and furniture legs onto foil or foam blocks, and move valuables to a dry room.

Notify and document

Tell your property manager or neighbors below you, then photograph every wet surface.

Water problems that fit a harbor community

Marina del Rey sits close to sea level and beside open water. During heavy rain, low spots, parking garages and ground-floor units are the places where stormwater can collect, and wind-driven rain can enter at windows and doors.

In an enclosed space, a leak can leave materials damp for a long time. That raises the risk of mold in closets, bathrooms and behind furniture, which is why mold remediation sits next to water removal in our service plan.

Water here can come from the ocean side as well as from pipes and roofs, so a call about a wet ground floor is never assumed to be a plumbing problem until a crew checks the source.
