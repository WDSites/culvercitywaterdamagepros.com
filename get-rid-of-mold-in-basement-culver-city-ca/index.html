How to Get Rid of Mold in a Basement in Culver City, CA

To get rid of mold in a basement, fix the water source, dry the space, remove what mold has damaged and keep the area dry. Small surface patches on hard materials can sometimes be cleaned by a careful homeowner. Growth in drywall, insulation or wood usually needs removal by a crew with containment. This guide covers the steps, the limits and the mistakes to avoid.

By the Culver City Water Damage Pros team · October 27, 2026 · Mold

Step one is always the water

Cleaning mold without fixing moisture is a temporary fix. The EPA's guidance is blunt: the key to mold control is moisture control. Find the source, whether it is a plumbing leak, a seep through a wall or slab, a clogged drain or condensation, and stop it.

Then dry the space. A dehumidifier, steady airflow and removal of wet materials all help. If walls are wet inside, the crew may open them to reach the framing. Once everything is dry, the mold problem becomes a cleaning and removal problem.

How professional removal works

A crew follows a sequence that keeps spores contained. The standard that guides this work is ANSI/IICRC S520.

  1. Inspect and plan. The crew locates the source and defines the affected area, then explains the scope and price.
  2. Set containment. Plastic barriers and negative air machines with HEPA filtration separate the work area.
  3. Remove damaged materials. Mold-damaged drywall, insulation and trim are cut out and bagged.
  4. Clean what remains. HEPA vacuuming and wiping clean framing and hard surfaces.
  5. Dry and verify. Materials are dried and, if you wish, an independent party tests afterward.

Common mistakes that make it worse

These errors appear again and again.

  • Painting over mold or using primer alone, which traps moisture.
  • Scrubbing dry mold without protection, which spreads spores.
  • Relying on bleach alone, which does not reach growth inside porous materials.
  • Running fans across growth, which blows spores into other rooms.
  • Skipping the moisture fix, so the mold comes back.

Safety and protective gear

Wear an N95 respirator or better, gloves and eye protection when cleaning small areas. Keep children and pets out of the room, close doors and avoid using the central air system while you work.

If you have health concerns, talk to a physician before doing the work. A contractor cannot give medical advice, and mold health questions belong to a doctor.

Keeping it from coming back

No one can promise mold will never return, because new moisture can restart the problem. The best protection is a dry space, with drainage that moves water away, working ventilation and quick attention to leaks.

After remediation, watch the area for new stains or smells. If the problem returns, the moisture source was not fully fixed. The water mitigation page explains how drying is measured, which helps confirm a space is dry before it is closed up.

What a cleanup day looks like for a homeowner

For a small hard-surface job, set up first. Put on a respirator, gloves and eye protection, cover vents, open a window if the weather allows and bag all cleaning materials before moving them through the house. Work from the cleaner area toward the dirtier area.

Wipe the surface with detergent and water, let it dry fully and watch it for a week. Do not use a vacuum without a HEPA filter, because ordinary vacuums can blow spores back into the room. If the spot returns, stop and call for an inspection.

Culver City: permits and where to ask

Cutting out drywall is not the same as removing a wall. The Building Safety Division's permit list names wall replacement or removal as work that needs a permit and minor sheetrock repair as work that does not. The division's page says its list is not exhaustive.

The division offers virtual appointments for questions about permits and building codes, and its phone number is (310) 253-5800. If your lower room is in Marina del Rey, where the county, not the city, handles permits, ask the county. The service areas page lists coverage.
