Flood Water Cleanup in Culver City, CA: A Homeowner Guide

Flood water cleanup starts with safety, not a mop. Stay out of water near electrical equipment, keep children and pets away, and assume floodwater is contaminated until a crew says otherwise. Once it is safe, the work is pumping out the water, removing materials that cannot be cleaned, washing and sanitizing the rest, and drying everything before it is rebuilt.

By the Culver City Water Damage Pros team · October 27, 2026 · Flood

Safety before cleanup

Floodwater can hide hazards. Power lines, submerged outlets, broken glass and sharp debris are all possible, and the water itself may carry bacteria from drains and the street. Do not wade through it without boots and gloves, and do not touch anything electrical while standing in water.

Call 911 for any electrical, gas or life safety risk before you call a restoration crew. Do not run the washing machine or flush toilets if sewage could be involved. Turn off the main water valve only if you can reach it without stepping in water.

What to throw out and what can be cleaned

Floodwater is normally treated as grossly contaminated under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. That changes what can be saved.

A cleanup sequence that works

Follow this order. It keeps you safe and prevents rework.

  1. Photograph first. Take photos and video of the water line and damaged items before anything is moved.
  2. Remove standing water. Pumps and extraction units pull the bulk of the water. A crew with a truck-mounted unit works faster than a wet vacuum.
  3. Remove soaked materials. Carpet, pad, insulation and drywall that were underwater are cut out and bagged.
  4. Wash and sanitize. Hard surfaces are washed with detergent and treated with an antimicrobial.
  5. Dry the structure. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry framing and slabs, and readings confirm the result.

When to call a professional

Call a crew when water came from outside, when it is more than a few inches deep, when sewage is involved or when you are unsure what is safe. Floodwater cleanup is physical work, and the risks of contamination and electrical hazards are real.

Most flood jobs also need drying equipment that homeowners do not own. An LGR dehumidifier and a set of air movers are far more effective than household fans. If you want to understand what cleanup involves, read about emergency water removal or water damage cleanup before you call.

Mold after floodwater

Floodwater leaves a lot of moisture behind, and mold needs moisture to grow. The EPA says water-damaged areas and items should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, which is why floodwater cleanup moves fast.

If your home stayed wet for days before cleanup began, ask for a moisture check and an inspection. Mold remediation deals with growth that has already started, and the work begins by finding the moisture source.

Protecting your health while you clean

Floodwater can carry sewage, chemicals and debris, so treat every wet surface as contaminated until a crew says otherwise. Wear waterproof gloves, boots and eye protection, and wash your hands often. Keep children, older adults and pets away from the work area.

Cuts and scrapes are the quickest way to pick up an infection, so cover any open skin and clean wounds right away. If you feel unwell after a cleanup, talk to a physician. A contractor cannot give medical advice.

Culver City flood facts worth knowing

The Culver City Fire Department says a Flood Watch means a flood is possible in your area, while a Flood Warning means one is occurring or will soon occur. It also says not to enter flooded subterranean garages or any below-grade part of a building, and that water as shallow as six inches deep may sweep a person away.

For sandbags, the department says residents may take up to 10 per household, self-serve, at the rear of the headquarters fire station at 9600 Culver Blvd. The Office of Emergency Management is at (310) 253-5900.
